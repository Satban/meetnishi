How to Tell If Ground Beef Is Bad

Ground beef can spoil quickly, and knowing how to spot the signs of spoilage is essential for keeping your meals safe and enjoyable. If your ground beef has an off smell, changes in color, or feels slimy, it's best to throw it out.

The Details

Ground beef should be stored in the refrigerator at a temperature of 40°F or below. When properly stored, raw ground beef can last for about 1 to 2 days in the fridge. If you’re not planning to use it right away, freezing is a good option, it can last in the freezer for 3 to 4 months. Keep in mind that while freezing preserves the meat, the quality may diminish after a few months.

The Science

Ground beef can go bad due to the growth of bacteria such as Salmonella and Escherichia coli (E. coli), which thrive in the temperature danger zone of 40°F to 140°F. These bacteria can develop from handling, packaging, or cross-contamination. Additionally, enzymes in the meat can break down its quality, leading to changes in color and texture.

Signs It's Gone Bad

Here are the main indicators that ground beef has spoiled:

  • Smell: If it has a sour or funky odor, it’s time to dispose of it.
  • Color: Fresh ground beef should be bright red to pink. If you see gray or brown patches, it might be losing its freshness.
  • Texture: If the meat is slimy to the touch or sticky, it’s best to throw it away.
  • Expiration Date: Always check for the sell-by or use-by date on the packaging.

Quick Reference

  • Store ground beef in the fridge for 1 to 2 days.
  • In the freezer, it lasts 3 to 4 months for best quality.
  • Always check for off smells, discolored appearance, or slimy texture.
