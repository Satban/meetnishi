Pork Storage: Fridge Longevity Explained

By the Nishi team
Notes on food, your kitchen, and feeling good.

Share:

Pork typically lasts 3 to 5 days in the fridge. Let's explore how to store it smartly.

Key Takeaways

  • Raw and cooked pork lasts 3 to 5 days in the fridge.
  • Store in airtight containers to maintain quality.
  • Watch for sour smells and slimy textures as signs of spoilage.
  • Always keep your fridge at or below 40°F.

How Long Does Pork Last in the Fridge?

Pork typically lasts about 3 to 5 days in the fridge. This timeframe applies to both raw and cooked pork, but storage conditions can play a significant role in freshness.

The Details

For raw pork, whether it's chops, roasts, or ground pork, aim to use it within 3 to 5 days after purchase. Cooked pork, on the other hand, can also be kept for about 3 to 4 days. If you're planning to keep it longer, it’s best to freeze bacon or cooked dishes containing pork; they can safely last in the freezer for several months!

When storing pork in the fridge, ensure it's in an airtight container or well-wrapped to maintain quality and prevent cross-contamination. Keeping your fridge temperature at or below 40°F helps slow the growth of harmful bacteria.

The Science

So why the timeframe? Bacteria grow quickly at temperatures between 40°F and 140°F, known as the danger zone. While pork is safe to consume for a bit after those initial days, it’s about minimizing risk from pathogens like Salmonella and Listeria. These microorganisms can multiply rapidly if the pork is not stored correctly.

Signs It's Gone Bad

To determine if pork has expired, trust your senses! Look out for:

  • Smell: A sour or off odor is a clear sign.
  • Appearance: Any discoloration or significant browning is a red flag.
  • Texture: If the surface feels sticky or slimy, it’s best to let it go.

Quick Reference

  • Raw Pork: 3 to 5 days in the fridge.
  • Cooked Pork: 3 to 4 days in the fridge.
  • Bacon: 1 week in the fridge.
  • Freezing: Cooked pork can last up to 3 months.
