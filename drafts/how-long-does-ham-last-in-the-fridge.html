How Long Does Ham Last in the Fridge

By the Nishi team
Notes on food, your kitchen, and feeling good.

How Long Does Ham Last in the Fridge

Ham typically lasts in the fridge for about 3 to 5 days once it's been cooked or opened. If it's unopened and stored properly, it may last a bit longer, given that it's within its sell-by date. The key to keeping ham fresh lies in how you store it and the conditions of your refrigerator.

The Details

For best results, keep ham in its original packaging or wrap it tightly in plastic wrap or aluminum foil. It's important to store ham in the coldest part of your fridge, usually near the back. Make sure your fridge is set at 40°F (4°C) or lower, as bacteria thrive in warmer environments.

Different types of ham have varying shelf lives. For instance, deli ham might last about 3 to 5 days after opening, while a whole, uncooked ham can last 1 to 2 weeks in the fridge if unopened. If you want to extend its life beyond that, consider freezing it. Cooked ham can last in the freezer for 1 to 2 months, while raw ham can go for up to 6 months.

The Science

So, why does ham have a limited shelf life? Bacteria, like Salmonella and Listeria, thrive in the refrigerator if the temperature isn't kept below 40°F. Even in refrigeration, enzymes can continue to break down the meat, changing its taste and texture over time. To prevent spoilage, always handle ham with clean hands and use sanitized surfaces.

Signs It's Gone Bad

When it comes to checking if ham has gone bad, there are a few things to look out for. First, check for any off smells or a slimy texture. Any discoloration can also be a warning sign. If it has developed an unusual odor or if you're just not sure, it's wise to err on the side of caution and discard it.

Quick Reference

  • Cooked Ham: 3 to 5 days in fridge
  • Unopened Raw Ham: 1 to 2 weeks
  • Deli Ham: 3 to 5 days after opening
  • Frozen Cooked Ham: 1 to 2 months
  • Frozen Raw Ham: Up to 6 months
