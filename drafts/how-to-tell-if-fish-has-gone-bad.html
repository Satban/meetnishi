How to Tell If Fish Has Gone Bad

By the Nishi team
Notes on food, your kitchen, and feeling good.

Share:

Fish can spoil quickly. Learn how to identify signs of spoilage to keep your meals safe and delicious.

  • Fresh fish lasts 1-2 days in the fridge.
  • Store cooked fish in the fridge for 3-4 days.
  • Signs of spoilage: smell, texture, and color.
  • Freeze raw fish for up to 6 months for longer storage.

How to Tell If Fish Has Gone Bad

Fish can spoil quickly, so it's important to know how to tell if it has gone bad. Look for signs like an off-smell, slimy texture, or a dull color.

The Details

Fish should be stored properly to stay fresh. If you're keeping raw fish, it’s best to consume it within 1-2 days when stored in the refrigerator at temperatures below 40°F. For cooked fish, you can typically keep it in the fridge for about 3-4 days. If you need to store it for longer, freezing is a great option; raw fish can last up to 6 months in the freezer, while cooked fish is safe for about 2-3 months.

The Science

Fish spoilage is often caused by bacteria, enzymes, and oxidation. Bacteria like Salmonella and Listeria thrive between 40°F and 140°F, which is known as the danger zone. Keeping fish at safe temperatures helps slow down spoilage.

Signs It's Gone Bad

Here are some clear indicators that fish has spoiled:

  • Smell: Fresh fish should have a mild ocean scent. If it smells sour, ammonia-like, or fishy, it’s likely gone bad.
  • Texture: Fresh fish should be firm to the touch. If it feels slimy or sticky, it may be a sign of spoilage.
  • Color: Look for vibrant, clear colors. If the flesh has turned dull or brown, that could indicate it has gone off.

Quick Reference

  • Store raw fish in the fridge for up to 1-2 days
  • Cooked fish lasts in the refrigerator for 3-4 days
  • Freeze raw fish for up to 6 months
  • Cooked fish can be frozen for 2-3 months
