Why Foods Affect Us Differently

By the Nishi team
Notes on food, your kitchen, and feeling good.

Why Foods Affect Us Differently

It’s not uncommon to share a meal with a friend, only to compare reactions later and realize you’re on completely different pages. While you could both enjoy a plate of spicy lentil curry, one might feel energized while the other grapples with digestive discomfort. What gives? It turns out our unique food experiences are shaped by an intricate interplay of factors, and it’s not just about taste.

Genetics: Our Biological Blueprint

First off, let’s talk genetics. Each person’s DNA can affect how they metabolize food. Some folks carry genes that influence how they process certain nutrients, like fats and carbs. Those variations can dictate whether your body thrives on a high-carb meal or if your friend feels sluggish after the same dish. Ever met someone who can pile on the cheese without a second thought? That might be genetic good luck on their part. The same food can trigger very different metabolic responses depending on our unique genetic makeup.

The Gut Connection: More Than Digestion

Your gut isn’t just a tube for food. It’s home to trillions of microorganisms (the microbiome) that play a crucial role in how your body interacts with food. Some people have a microbiome that thrives on fiber-rich foods, while others may have a different community that favors protein. This subtle ecosystem can influence everything from digestion to mood and immunity. When you bite into that creamy avocado, one of you might feel satisfied and full of energy, while the other could end up feeling bloated due to differences in gut bacteria.

Mindset and Personal Experience: The Psychological Layer

Let’s not forget the psychological aspects at play. Our emotions, memories, and how we view food can shape our reactions. If you associate chocolate with comfort and warmth, a piece of dark chocolate might brighten your mood. On the flip side, if someone has had a past negative experience with a specific food, even the smell of it can trigger an unwanted response. This mental and emotional connection to food is just as valid as the physical one and can lead to very different experiences even when sharing a meal.

It's fascinating to realize that our relationships with food are as unique as our fingerprints. Being aware of how you and others respond can help in making more personal, tuned-in choices about what to eat. Perhaps that means opting for something lighter when dining with a friend who’s sensitive to rich foods or being daring with spices if you know your gut can handle it.

Common Questions

Why does my friend love spicy food while I can't handle it?

Spicy food tolerance can stem from genetic differences and personal experiences. Some people have more pain receptors in their mouths, while others might have trained their palates to enjoy heat over time.

Can my gut health really change how I digest food?

Absolutely! The health and balance of your gut bacteria can significantly affect digestion and nutrient absorption, shaping how you react to different meals.

How much does my mood affect my food choices?

Your mood can play a substantial role in food choices and how you feel after eating. Emotional associations with certain foods can lead to different experiences, regardless of the food's nutritional value.
