How Long Does Cooked Pork Last?

Cooked pork generally lasts for 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator and 2 to 3 months in the freezer if stored correctly. Proper storage is key to keeping your meals safe and tasty.

The Details

When you cook pork, any leftover portions should be refrigerated within two hours of cooking to ensure safety. Use airtight containers or wrap it tightly in aluminum foil or plastic wrap to keep out moisture and odors. If you plan to keep it longer, the freezer is your best friend. Just make sure to label your packages with the date, so you know when to use them.

The Science

Cooked pork can spoil due to the growth of bacteria and the activity of enzymes that naturally occur in meat. Bacteria thrive in the "danger zone" between 40°F and 140°F. Pathogens like Salmonella and Listeria can develop if pork isn't stored properly. Additionally, oxidation can cause a decline in flavor and quality, so keeping it sealed tight helps slow that process.

Signs It's Gone Bad

If your cooked pork has an off smell, a slimy texture, or a color change (such as gray or green spots), it's best to err on the side of caution and throw it away. If you’re ever in doubt, trust your nose and eyes.

Quick Reference

  • Fridge: 3 to 4 days
  • Freezer: 2 to 3 months
  • Storage method: Airtight containers or tightly wrapped
